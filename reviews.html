PARTS REVIEWS

Straight opinions on the parts people actually ask us about — what held up, what didn't, and what we'd buy again. No sponsored placements.

Ceramic Brake Pads

BRAKES
★★★★★

Quiet, low dust, and they bite predictably from cold. Not the cheapest option on the shelf, but they don't glaze as fast as the budget semi-metallics we've tried, and your wheels stay cleaner between washes.

Good for: daily commuters, people tired of black dust on their rims.

Standard Oil Filter

ENGINE
★★★★★

Does the job for a normal oil change interval, but the housing is thinner than the OEM part — fine if you're not overtightening it by hand at every change. We wouldn't stretch this one past the interval on the box.

Good for: routine changes on a budget. Skip it if you're doing extended intervals.

AGM Battery

ELECTRICAL
★★★★★

Cranked strong through a cold snap that killed a cheaper flooded battery in the same lot. Costs more up front, but if your car sits outside or you run a lot of accessories, it's worth the difference.

Good for: cold climates, cars with a lot of electronics, anyone who's been stranded once already.

All-Season Touring Tires

TIRES
★★★★★

Quiet on the highway and predictable in light rain and snow. They're not a substitute for real winter tires if you're somewhere that gets serious snow, but for most daily driving they're a solid all-rounder that wears evenly.

Good for: daily commuters in mixed climates. Skip if you get heavy snow — get dedicated winters instead.

LED Headlight Bulbs

LIGHTING
★★★★★

Noticeably brighter and whiter than the halogens they replaced, but fit varies a lot by vehicle — some housings need an adapter, and a few owners reported flickering until they grounded the wiring properly. Worth it once installed right.

Good for: anyone tired of dim factory halogens. Check your specific bulb housing before buying.

Full-Synthetic Motor Oil

FLUIDS
★★★★★

Costs more per change than conventional oil, but it holds up better under heat and lets you stretch the interval if your manual allows it — which usually evens out the price difference over a year of driving.

Good for: newer engines, hot climates, anyone doing highway miles. Overkill for a car you're about to sell.
